What I read

The bookshelf

110+ books read. These are the 27 that stayed with me. Tap any spine to see what I took from it.

    See all 27 books as a list

    Habits & productivity

    • Atomic Habits by James Clear. Focus on your trajectory, not your current results. Tiny changes, repeated day after day, compound into big ones.
    • Slow Productivity by Cal Newport. Do fewer things, work at a natural pace, and obsess over quality.
    • Ultralearning by Scott H. Young. Learn by doing, then drill the weak spots. Testing yourself beats rereading every time.
    • Protocols by Andrew Huberman. Sleep, light, movement and food are the foundation every other habit stands on.
    • Hidden Potential by Adam Grant. Character skills, not natural talent, decide how far you go.
    • Creativity by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi. Flow, deep engagement in hard work, is where enjoyment and creativity meet.
    • Principles by Ray Dalio. Principles connect your values to your actions. For every one, ask: is it actually true?

    Marketing & persuasion

    • Influence by Robert B. Cialdini. We say yes on mental shortcuts like reciprocity, scarcity, consistency, social proof, liking and authority. Know them, and keep the customer’s best interest at heart.
    • Never Split the Difference by Chris Voss. Don’t split the difference. Listen closely, mirror what you hear, and stay calm enough that the other side keeps talking.
    • Supercommunicators by Charles Duhigg. Every conversation is practical, emotional or social. Figure out which one you’re in.
    • $100M Offers by Alex Hormozi. Make an offer so good people feel foolish saying no.
    • Marketing Made Simple by Donald Miller. The customer is the hero of the story. The brand is the guide.
    • Marketing 6.0 by Philip Kotler et al.. Marketing is moving toward immersive experiences that blend physical and digital.
    • This I Know by Terry O’Reilly. The best marketing is a clear strategy told as a good story.

    Money & investing

    • The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel. Getting rich is simpler than staying rich. Stay humble, respect the role of luck, and know when enough is enough.
    • The Intelligent Investor by Benjamin Graham. Treat stocks as businesses, and always leave a margin of safety.
    • The Snowball by Alice Schroeder. Patience and compounding, applied for decades, outperform brilliance applied in bursts.
    • Poor Charlie’s Almanack by Charles T. Munger. Patience and focus are an investor’s greatest strengths. Stay inside what you understand, and own your mistakes fast.
    • Think and Grow Rich by Napoleon Hill. Define the goal precisely, write it down, read it twice a day, and surround yourself with smart people.

    Lives & leadership

    • Steve Jobs by Walter Isaacson. Make fewer products and make them simple. A seamless experience is the product.
    • Elon Musk by Walter Isaacson. Question every requirement, then delete whatever part or step you can.
    • The Ride of a Lifetime by Robert Iger. Bold, well-timed bets, like Pixar and Marvel, can transform a company.
    • A Promised Land by Barack Obama. You don’t choose the time; the time chooses you. Be ready when it does.
    • Business Adventures by John Brooks. The same human mistakes show up in business again and again.

    Mind & meaning

    • The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F*ck by Mark Manson. Everything worth doing comes with struggle, so choose the struggle that’s right for you, and say no to the rest.
    • Wisdom Takes Work by Ryan Holiday. Wisdom isn’t knowing more. It’s practising what you already know.
    • Values by Mark Carney. Markets are powerful tools, but they need values to point them somewhere good.

    Many of these ideas found their way into Enjoy Productivity, ten habits drawn from the best of what I’ve read and tested.
